Quantifying the Effects of Poaching on Four Partridge Species in Dera Ismail Khan, Pakistan

Source URL: https://researcherslinks.com/current-issues/Quantifying-the-Effects-of-Poaching/20%E7%B5%85%E5%B8%91%EF%BD%A8%E3%82%86%E8%90%A9%EF%BE%8Fsubscription/1/12697

FACTUAL PARAPHRASE

The study examined the effects of poaching on four partridge species in Dera Ismail Khan, Pakistan. Researchers conducted field surveys and structured questionnaire interviews from March 2022 through December 2023 to assess abundance, diversity, captive breeding, poaching impacts and income associated with raising partridges.

Point-count surveys recorded 4,084 birds: 1,204 Chukar partridges, 1,528 Grey partridges, 510 See-see partridges and 842 Black francolins. The researchers also interviewed 213 respondents.

The study found differences among species in abundance, sex ratios and diversity measures. Respondents identified predators, disease and human activity as threats. Captive breeding generally involved mating from March to May and egg laying from April to June, with breeding performance varying by species.

Among respondents who raised partridges, income generation was the most frequently reported purpose at 51.92%, followed by hobby at 13.46% and keeping birds as pets at 11.54%. The authors estimated that partridge raising generated approximately 18 million to 22 million Pakistani rupees annually.

The article was published in Pakistan Journal of Zoology, volume 58, issue 3, pages 1257-1270. It is an open-access article distributed under a Creative Commons Attribution license.
